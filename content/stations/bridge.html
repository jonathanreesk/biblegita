Know the Bible first

In Athens, Paul began with what his hearers already knew and quoted one of their own poets before pointing to the resurrection (Acts 17:22-31). The lesson is the order, not the quoting: start where your friend is, then bring them to Christ. You do not need to master the Gita to do that. Know the Bible well, and know why you believe it, because that is where your confidence rests.

Add from the Gita only what helps: a few verses your friend actually cares about, or simply ask them which ones matter most and listen. It is fine to say, "I do not know that verse well yet. Tell me about it."

Keep it fair

These four challenges are drawn from Christian apologetics and target specific schools, mostly Advaita. Hindu philosophers have answered them for centuries, and some of their replies (on original sin, or on whether an infinite past is possible) are debated among Christian thinkers too. Present the arguments as questions to explore together, and expect to learn from your friend's answers.

Christ above all

Every longing the Gita reaches for, to be loved, to be free, to see justice done, to have God near, finds its Yes in Jesus Christ. All the promises of God are Yes in Him (2 Corinthians 1:20).
