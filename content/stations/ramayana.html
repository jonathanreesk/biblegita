The Hindu texts in depth

The Ramayana

The Ramayana is the second great Hindu epic. It is shorter and simpler than the Mahabharata, and for many Hindus it is the most loved story of all.

Does it fit?The storyIts themeQuestions

Does it fit inside the Mahabharata?

No. The Ramayana is a separate epic, traditionally credited to the sage Valmiki. The Mahabharata knows it, and retells a short version of it (the Ramopakhyana) to comfort Yudhishthira in exile. So the two epics are neighbours, not parent and child. Many scholars think the Ramayana took its main shape a little earlier than the Mahabharata, though both grew over centuries.

The story in short

  1. 1Rama is sent away

    Rama, prince of Ayodhya, is about to be crowned. Because of a promise his father made, he is sent into the forest for fourteen years instead. He obeys without complaint. His wife Sita and his brother Lakshmana go with him.

  2. 2Sita is taken

    Ravana, the ten-headed king of Lanka, kidnaps Sita.

  3. 3The search and the war

    Rama gathers allies, among them the faithful monkey Hanuman. They cross to Lanka, and Rama defeats Ravana.

  4. 4Return and rule

    Rama returns to Ayodhya and is crowned. His reign is remembered as the ideal kingdom, "Rama Rajya". Later versions tell of Sita being tested or sent away, and these parts are still debated by Hindus today.

What it is about

Theme

Dharma lived out: the ideal son, husband, brother, king and servant. Rama keeps his word even when it costs him everything.

The problem

Evil and disorder, in the form of Ravana, threaten the world.

The way out

Rama, an avatar of Vishnu, comes to earth, defeats evil and restores order.

The goal

A just world, and for the devoted, a life of loyal love like Hanuman's.

Notice the difference from the Mahabharata. In the Mahabharata no one is fully good, and the story is full of hard choices. In the Ramayana the hero is held up as the model of goodness. Rama is also worshipped as God, so many Hindus see him as the nearest picture of a good God coming down to rescue.

A Christian view

What the Ramayana gets right

It shows a deep human longing: for a good king, for a rescuer who comes down, and for evil to be defeated. Many Christians find real beauty in Rama's faithfulness and in Hanuman's devotion.

Where the Bible goes further

The Bible says that rescuer really came. Jesus did not come to defeat an enemy in a distant land, but to deal with sin in every heart. He "bore our sins in his body on the tree" (1 Peter 2:24), and He rose, so He is alive and not only remembered.

Two honest questions

Is Rama a historical person?

Many Hindus say yes, and some hold that he lived in the treta age, hundreds of thousands of years ago. Others read the Ramayana as a sacred story that teaches truth rather than as history. Historians have no outside records of Rama or Lanka. The Gospels are different: they were written within living memory of Jesus, and name real rulers and places (Luke 3:1-2).

Does Rama's goodness answer the problem of sin?

Rama keeps his word and does what is right. That is a beautiful picture of what a person ought to be. But it does not say what happens to those who have not lived like Rama. The Bible says all have fallen short (Romans 3:23), and that Jesus is the one who lived without falling short and gives His life for us (2 Corinthians 5:21).

Christ above all

Rama is honoured because he kept his word at great cost. Jesus kept His perfectly, all the way to the cross, and then rose again. The longing for a perfect king who rescues is answered in Him (Revelation 19:16).
