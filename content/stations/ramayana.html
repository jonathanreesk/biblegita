The two stories

The Ramayana

The Ramayana, credited to the sage Valmiki, is the second Hindu epic, and the story most Hindus know best. Rama is worshipped as an avatar of Vishnu, so many Hindus compare him with Jesus. The comparison fails at every point that matters.

The story in short

  1. 1Rama is sent away

    Rama, prince of Ayodhya, is about to be crowned. Because of a promise his father made, he is sent into the forest for fourteen years instead. He obeys without complaint. His wife Sita and his brother Lakshmana go with him.

  2. 2Sita is taken

    Ravana, the ten-headed king of Lanka, kidnaps Sita.

  3. 3The search and the war

    Rama gathers allies, among them the faithful monkey Hanuman. They cross to Lanka, and Rama defeats Ravana.

  4. 4Return and rule

    Rama returns to Ayodhya and is crowned. Hindus call his reign the ideal kingdom, "Rama Rajya". Later versions tell of Sita being tested or sent away, and these parts are still debated by Hindus today.

What it is about

Theme

Dharma lived out. Rama is presented as the ideal son, husband, brother, king and servant, who keeps his word whatever it costs.

The problem

Evil and disorder, in the form of Ravana, threaten the world.

The way out

Rama, an avatar of Vishnu, comes to earth, defeats evil and restores order.

The goal

A just world, and for the devoted, a life of loyal love like Hanuman's.

In the Mahabharata no one is fully good. In the Ramayana the hero is held up as the model of dharma, and he is worshipped as God. So the questions are simple: is the story true, is the hero really good, and can he save anyone?

Where it goes wrong

Did it happen?
The Ramayana

No evidence, and many versionsTradition places Rama in the treta age, hundreds of thousands of years ago. There is no outside record of his kingdom, his war or Lanka. The story is told differently by Valmiki, by Tulsidas, by the Tamil poet Kamban and across Southeast Asia, and the versions disagree even on how it ends.

The Bible

Real people, real placesThe Gospels were written within living memory of Jesus and name the rulers of the day: Tiberius Caesar, Pontius Pilate and Herod (Luke 3:1-2). Jesus was seen alive after His death by more than five hundred people, most still living when Paul wrote (1 Corinthians 15:6).

Is the hero without fault?
The Ramayana

NoRama is called the perfect man, yet he shoots the monkey king Vali from hiding (Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda). He makes Sita prove her purity by walking into fire (Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda), and later sends her away, pregnant, into the forest because of gossip in the city (Ramayana, Uttara Kanda). Hindus themselves still argue over these scenes.

The Bible

One man without sin"He committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth" (1 Peter 2:22). When a woman was dragged before Him in shame, Jesus did not send her away. He sent her accusers away (John 8:10-11).

Who is the enemy?
The Ramayana

A demon king, far awayEvil is Ravana in Lanka. Defeat him and order returns.

The Bible

Sin, in every heartThe problem is not only out there. It is in us: "all have sinned, and fall short of the glory of God" (Romans 3:23). No war in a distant land can fix that.

An avatar is not an incarnation

Many Hindus say that Jesus is one more avatar like Rama and Krishna. The two ideas are very different.

How often?
Avatar

Again and againVishnu is "born in every age" whenever right living declines (Gita 4:7-8). Rama and Krishna are two of many.

Jesus

Once for allJesus "has been revealed at the end of the ages to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself" (Hebrews 9:26). It never needs to happen again.

Why does he come?
Avatar

To destroy the wickedKrishna says he comes "for the protection of the good, for the destruction of the wicked" (Gita 4:8).

Jesus

To die for the wicked"While we were yet sinners, Christ died for us" (Romans 5:8). He came for the very people an avatar comes to destroy.

What does he do with sin?
Avatar

Nothing is paidRama and Krishna fight evil people. Neither dies to take away anyone's sin.

Jesus

He carries it"He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree" (1 Peter 2:24).

What happens after?
Avatar

He leaves, and the cycle turnsRama returns to Vishnu's heaven. The ages roll on, and another avatar will be needed.

Jesus

He rose, and He reigns"I am alive forever and ever. Amen. I have the keys of Death and of Hades" (Revelation 1:18). He will return once, to end evil for good.

Expect this reply

  • "The Ramayana is about ideals, not history." Many Hindus say it does not matter whether it happened, because it teaches how to live.
  • "The hard scenes were added later, or have a deeper meaning." Some scholars do think the last book was added later.

Your strongest answer

Then the Ramayana can show you what a good person ought to be, but it cannot rescue you. An ideal cannot forgive sin, and a hero who has to be defended is not the perfect man. Rama shows how far short we fall. Jesus is the one who never fell short, and then took the place of those who did.

What it asks, and what the Bible announces

The Ramayana asks

What does the perfect king look like? It answers with a hero who wins a war, makes mistakes, and leaves.

The Bible announces

The true King came. He wore a crown of thorns, died for His enemies and rose again, and He is "King of Kings, and Lord of Lords" (Revelation 19:16).

Christ above all

Hindus honor Rama for keeping his word. Jesus kept His word perfectly, all the way to the cross, and the cost was for us. He did not destroy His enemies. He died for them, and He lives to make them His friends (John 15:13-15).
