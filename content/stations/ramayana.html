The two stories

The Ramayana

The Ramayana, credited to the sage Valmiki, is the second Hindu epic, and the story most Hindus know best. Rama is worshipped as an avatar of Vishnu, so many Hindus compare him with Jesus. Put the two to the same test, and the comparison does not hold.

The story in short

  1. 1Rama is sent away

    Rama, prince of Ayodhya, is about to be crowned. Because of a promise his father made, he is sent into the forest for fourteen years instead. He obeys without complaint. His wife Sita and his brother Lakshmana go with him.

  2. 2Sita is taken

    Ravana, the ten-headed king of Lanka, kidnaps Sita.

  3. 3The search and the war

    Rama gathers allies, among them the faithful monkey Hanuman. They cross to Lanka, and Rama defeats Ravana.

  4. 4Return and rule

    Rama returns to Ayodhya and is crowned. Hindus call his reign the ideal kingdom, "Rama Rajya". Later versions tell of Sita being tested or sent away, and these parts are still debated by Hindus today.

What it is about

ThemeDharma lived out. Rama is presented as the ideal son, husband, brother, king and servant, who keeps his word whatever it costs.
  1. The problemEvil and disorder, in the form of Ravana, threaten the world.
  2. The way outRama, an avatar of Vishnu, comes to earth, defeats evil and restores order.
  3. The goalA just world, and for the devoted, a life of loyal love like Hanuman's.

In the Mahabharata no one is fully good. In the Ramayana the hero is held up as the model of dharma, and he is worshipped as God. So the questions are simple: is the story true, is the hero really good, and can he save anyone?

Expect this reply

  • "The Ramayana is about ideals, not history." Many Hindus say it does not matter whether it happened, because it teaches how to live.
  • "The hard scenes were added later, or have a deeper meaning." Some scholars do think the last book was added later.

Your strongest answer

Then the Ramayana can show you what a good person ought to be, but it cannot rescue you. An ideal cannot forgive sin, and a hero who has to be defended is not the perfect man. Rama shows how far short we fall. Jesus is the one who never fell short, and then took the place of those who did.

How it ends

Most people know the Ramayana as the story of a rescue and a coronation. Valmiki's last book goes further, and its ending is a sad one.

  1. Uttara KandaSita is sent away

    Because people in the city gossip about her time in Lanka, Rama has the pregnant Sita left in the forest. She gives birth to twin sons, Lava and Kusha, at Valmiki's hermitage.

  2. Years laterSita returns to the earth

    Rama meets his sons and asks Sita to prove her purity once more in front of everyone. Instead she calls on the Earth, her mother, to take her if she has been faithful. The ground opens, and she is gone.

  3. The end of Rama's reignInto the river

    Rama rules on in grief. Time itself comes to tell him his work is done. Bound by a promise, he sends away his faithful brother Lakshmana, who gives up his life in the river Sarayu. Then Rama walks into the same river with the people of Ayodhya and returns to his form as Vishnu. The age of Treta ends, and the world moves on to a darker age.

Many Hindus never hear this ending. Tulsidas's Ramcharitmanas, the best-known retelling, ends with Rama crowned and Sita at his side, and leaves the rest out. So even the ending depends on which Ramayana you read.

What it asks, and what the Bible announces

The Ramayana asks

What does the perfect king look like? It answers with a hero who wins a war, makes mistakes, and leaves.

The Bible announces

The true King came. He wore a crown of thorns, died for His enemies and rose again, and He is "King of Kings, and Lord of Lords" (Revelation 19:16).

Christ above all

Hindus honor Rama for keeping his word. Jesus kept His word perfectly, all the way to the cross, and the cost was for us. He did not destroy His enemies. He died for them, and He lives to make them His friends (John 15:13-15).
