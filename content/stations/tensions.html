Can the texts be trusted?

Hard passages in both texts

Every long religious text has passages that readers find hard to fit together. A fair conversation names the difficulties in your own text first, then asks how your friend handles theirs.This step looks honestly at the hard passages in the Bible, and then at the Hindu texts. The Hindu questions below are ones Hindu teachers and readers discuss among themselves. The Christian ones are the objections you will hear.

Hard questions Hindu readers ask

War and non-violence

Krishna urges Arjuna to fight, telling him there is "nothing higher for a Kshatriya than a righteous war" (Gita 2:31) and warning of sin if he refuses (Gita 2:33). Yet ahimsa, non-harm, is among Hinduism's highest values. Some read the battle as an inner struggle. Others take it literally.

Which path saves?

The Gita names a path of knowledge and a path of action (Gita 3:3), and teaches devotion elsewhere. Great commentators disagree over which one the Gita finally puts first. Shankara stressed knowledge, and Ramanuja stressed devotion.

Keep your duty, or leave all duties?

"Better is one's own duty, though devoid of merit, than the duty of another" (Gita 3:35). Yet the Gita ends: "Abandon all duties and take refuge in Me alone" (Gita 18:66). Readers debate how the two fit together.

The Vedas honored and criticized

Krishna criticizes those who take pleasure in the "flowery speech" of the Vedas (Gita 2:42), then calls himself the one "to be known by all the Vedas" (Gita 15:15).

Where the Hindu texts differ from one another

The Hindu texts were written over about two thousand years by different schools, and they often give different answers to the same question. Here are two of the biggest.

Who is the highest God?
  • Early Rig VedaIndra, Agni and Varuna, each praised as highest
  • UpanishadsBrahman, beyond any form or quality
  • GitaKrishna, a personal Lord
  • Vishnu PuranasVishnu, with Brahma born from his navel (Bhagavata Purana 3.8)
  • Shiva PuranasShiva, the endless pillar of fire (Shiva Purana)
  • Goddess textsThe Mother, above all three
The BibleOne God, from the first page to the last: "I am Yahweh, and there is no one else" (Isaiah 45:5).
How did the world begin?
  • Rig Veda 10.129No one knows, perhaps not even the highest one
  • Rig Veda 10.121From a golden womb or germ
  • Rig Veda 10.90From the body of a sacrificed cosmic person
  • UpanishadsIt comes out of Brahman
  • PuranasBrahma creates it again and again, age after age
The Bible"In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth" (Genesis 1:1).

They differ in smaller ways too:

  • Meat and non-harm. The Vedas describe animal sacrifice, while later texts praise ahimsa, non-harm, as the highest duty.
  • Images of God. Some Hindus read Yajurveda 40.9 as a warning against worshipping created things, while the Puranas and Agamas give detailed rules for temple images.
  • Rama and Krishna. Rama keeps his word whatever it costs, while Krishna advises the Pandavas to break the rules of war.

Expect this reply

  • "These are not contradictions, only layers and paths." Many Hindus hold this. But layers that disagree about who God is cannot all be true about God.

Hard questions Christian readers face

Two family trees for Jesus

Matthew and Luke list different ancestors between David and Jesus. Matthew traces the line through David's son Solomon (Matthew 1:6) and names Joseph's father as Jacob (Matthew 1:16). Luke traces it through David's son Nathan (Luke 3:31) and calls Joseph "the son of Heli" (Luke 3:23). Matthew also starts with Abraham and counts forward, while Luke counts back all the way to Adam (Luke 3:38). Common reply: Matthew follows the legal line through Joseph, the royal heir, and Luke most likely follows Mary's family line. Ancient genealogies had different purposes.

Two accounts of Judas's death

Matthew says Judas hanged himself (Matthew 27:5). Acts says he fell headlong and burst open (Acts 1:18). Common reply: the accounts can describe two stages of one event, with the hanging first and the fall after. Not every reader finds this tidy.

Resurrection details differ

The four Gospels differ on who went to the tomb: two women in Matthew (Matthew 28:1), three in Mark (Mark 16:1), several in Luke (Luke 24:10), and only Mary Magdalene is named in John (John 20:1). They also differ on who met them: one angel in Matthew (Matthew 28:2), a young man in Mark (Mark 16:5), two men in Luke (Luke 24:4), and two angels in John (John 20:12). Common reply: each writer selects from a larger event. All agree that the tomb was empty, that women were the first witnesses, and that Jesus was alive. Paul lists the witnesses in 1 Corinthians 15:3-8.

Faith or works?

Paul: "a man is justified by faith apart from the works of the law" (Romans 3:28). James: "by works, a man is justified, and not only by faith" (James 2:24). Common reply: they use "justified" and "works" for different things, and both say that real faith produces good works (Ephesians 2:8-10).

Command to destroy, command to love

God commands Israel to destroy certain Canaanite cities (Deuteronomy 20:16-17), while Jesus says "love your enemies" (Matthew 5:44). Common reply: the first was a one-time judgment on particular nations at a particular time, never a standing order, and Jesus fulfills the Old Testament and does not repeat that command. Christians still find it hard, and you can say so.

Expect this reply

  • "Those are proof of human editing." Notice that the differences are about details. Independent witnesses rarely match word for word, and identical accounts would suggest they were coordinated.

Your strongest answer

A text can have hard passages and still be true at its center. The question is what each text stakes everything on. The Bible stakes everything on one public event: "If Christ has not been raised, then our preaching is in vain, and your faith also is in vain" (1 Corinthians 15:14). The difficulties above are about details around that event, not about whether Jesus died and rose. Gentleness is the point. You are not scoring points against a text your friend loves. You are showing that you have read yours with the same honesty you ask of theirs.

With the reliability of the texts and the hard questions now laid out, the next step turns to the heart of the difference: how sin is forgiven.

Christ above all

Hard passages do not move the center. Christ is the key to all Scripture (Luke 24:27), and He does not change: Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today and forever (Hebrews 13:8).
