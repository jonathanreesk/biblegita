The questions · Challenge 2

Karma and the endless chain

Logical issue: infinite regress

The Hindu claim

Present circumstances result from past actions. The soul has passed through countless births, and the cycle of rebirth (samsara) has no beginning. Krishna tells Arjuna that both of them have had many births.

Gita 4:5Gita 2:22Gita 8:16

The apologetic argument

  1. Karma explains suffering in this life by actions in a previous life.
  2. That life is explained by an earlier one, without end.
  3. An explanation that is always postponed never actually explains.
  4. So karma describes a mechanism but cannot account for the first moral act or for why the system exists at all.
Genesis 1:1Hebrews 9:27Acts 17:24-26

Expect this reply

  • "A beginningless series is not a contradiction." Classical Vedanta (Brahma Sutra 2.1.35-36) argues that samsara must be beginningless, otherwise the first births would be unequal for no reason, making God unjust.
  • "God oversees karma." Theistic schools say Ishvara dispenses the fruits of action, so the system has a ground.

Your strongest answer

The Vedanta reply is clever: it uses karma to protect God from blame for inequality. Ask whether it succeeds. If each inequality is explained by a prior one forever, the inequality is never explained, only pushed back. Also, an actual infinite number of past lives would have to be completed one by one to reach today, which many philosophers argue is impossible. The Bible names a starting point: a good creation, a real fall and a God who enters history in Jesus Christ, through whom all things were made, to deal with sin rather than leaving us to work it off.

Christ above all

The chain of causes needs a first cause and a final answer. Jesus Christ is both: all things were made through Him (John 1:3), and in Him all things hold together (Colossians 1:17).
