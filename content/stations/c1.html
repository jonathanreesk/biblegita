The questions · Challenge 1

Monism and the personal God

Logical issue: equivocation (one word used in two senses)

Is love at the very center of reality? Advaita and the Bible give different answers. Advaita says the highest reality has no qualities at all, love included. The Bible says love is what God is.

The Hindu claim

In Advaita (Shankara's school) there are two ways of speaking about God. Nirguna Brahman, "without qualities", is ultimate reality as it truly is: one, with no personality, will or love. Saguna Brahman, also called Ishvara, "with qualities", is that same reality as it appears through Maya: a personal Lord who creates, loves and receives worship. Devotion to the Lord is real and valued, but it belongs to the everyday level. At the highest level the worshipper, the Lord and love itself merge into the one reality. The Gita calls Maya God's own divine power, hard to cross. The classic source for this view is the Upanishads, in lines like "Tat tvam asi", "That you are" (Chandogya Upanishad 6.8.7).

The apologetic argument

  1. If ultimate reality has no attributes, love and justice are not ultimately real.
  2. The word "love" is then used in two senses: real at the human level, unreal at the ultimate one.
  3. Personal relationships need a personal foundation that is eternal.
  4. So Advaita cannot ground the love it values. The Trinity can, because love exists within God before anything is made.

Expect this reply

  • "Not all Hindus are Advaitins." Ramanuja and Madhva insisted God is personal with real qualities. The Gita itself is deeply personal: Krishna speaks as "I" and tells Arjuna he is dear to him (18:64-65).
  • "Maya is not simply unreal." Advaitins say the world is neither real nor unreal (anirvachaniya), so love is fully real at the level we live in.

Your strongest answer

Agree that the Gita's bhakti verses are personal, then ask: before anything was created, whom did a personal God love? Jesus speaks of the Father loving Him "before the foundation of the world" (John 17:24). A single-person God needs creation to have an object of love. Dvaita answers with eternal souls alongside God, which makes souls uncreated and God dependent on them. Only a God who is three persons in one being has love that is complete in Himself. And that love has come to us in Jesus Christ, who is Himself God the Son: if you want to know whether God loves you, look at the cross. For an Advaitin, test the "levels" answer by asking: if love disappears at the highest level, is it really ultimate?

Christ above all

Love is not only an idea at the center of reality. It has a face. The Son, who was with the Father before the world began, came and made God known (John 1:18). If you want to know whether God loves you, look at Jesus Christ.
