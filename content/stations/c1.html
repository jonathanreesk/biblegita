The questions · Challenge 1

Monism and the personal God

Logical issue: equivocation

The Hindu claim

In Advaita (Shankara's school), ultimate reality is Brahman, one and without qualities. The personal Lord (Saguna Brahman) and the distinction between souls belong to the level of Maya. The Gita itself speaks of Maya as divine and hard to cross.

Gita 7:14Gita 2:16Gita 13:12

The apologetic argument

  1. If ultimate reality has no attributes, love and justice are not ultimately real.
  2. The word "love" is then used in two senses: real at the human level, unreal at the ultimate one.
  3. Personal relationships need a personal foundation that is eternal.
  4. So Advaita cannot ground the love it values. The Trinity can, because love exists within God before anything is made.
1 John 4:8John 17:24John 1:1

Expect this reply

  • "Not all Hindus are Advaitins." Ramanuja and Madhva insisted God is personal with real qualities. The Gita itself is deeply personal: Krishna speaks as "I" and tells Arjuna he is dear to him (18:64-65).
  • "Maya is not simply unreal." Advaitins say the world is neither real nor unreal (anirvachaniya), so love is fully real at the level we live in.

Your strongest answer

Agree that the Gita's bhakti verses are personal, then ask: before anything was created, whom did a personal God love? A single-person God needs creation to have an object of love. Dvaita answers with eternal souls alongside God, which makes souls uncreated and God dependent on them. Only a God who is three persons in one being has love that is complete in Himself. For an Advaitin, press the "levels" answer: if love dissolves at the highest level, it is not ultimate.
