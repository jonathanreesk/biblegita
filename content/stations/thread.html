The cross · The Bible's thread

One thread through the whole Bible

The Bible is one finished text, compiled from 66 writings by about 40 authors in three languages over roughly 1,500 years. Yet Jesus told two travelers that all of it, "Moses and all the Prophets", speaks of Him (Luke 24:27). The thread that holds it together is hope: in a world that turned from God and is full of sin, suffering and death, God promises to rescue, and He keeps the promise.

Follow it through the Bible

The Law (Genesis to Deuteronomy)The promise comes at the darkest moment, right after the first rebellion: a descendant of the woman will crush the serpent (Genesis 3:15). God then promises Abraham that through his family all nations will be blessed (Genesis 12:3).
↓
The History (Joshua to Esther)Kings and people fail again and again. God promises David a descendant whose kingdom will last forever (2 Samuel 7:12-13).
↓
Wisdom and poetry (Job to Song of Solomon)Honest cries about suffering and longing for a rescuer: "I know that my Redeemer lives" (Job 19:25).
↓
The Prophets (Isaiah to Malachi)Hard warnings and great hope together: a child who will reign (Isaiah 9:6-7), a new heart for God's people (Ezekiel 36:26), and death swallowed up forever (Isaiah 25:8).
↓
The Gospels (Matthew to John)The promised rescuer arrives. Jesus dies to forgive sin and rises, and the powers of darkness are defeated.
↓
Acts and the LettersWhat the rescue means: through Christ God reconciles "all things" to Himself (Colossians 1:19-20), and He shares in our humanity "that through death he might bring to nothing him who had the power of death, that is, the devil" (Hebrews 2:14).
↓
RevelationThe promise is kept in full. Evil is finished, and God lives with His people.

Where the thread ends

The Bible ends with a final event that answers its first chapters. Sin is defeated, Satan is destroyed, death is destroyed, and God and His people live together in a renewed world. Notice how the last pages mirror the first.

The gospel

The good news is not only that you can be forgiven, but that the whole world is being rescued and made new, and that you are invited to know God and live with Him in it.

The claim at the center

The Bible's answer to a world of sin, evil and death is a person. Jesus Christ has defeated sin, Satan and death by His cross and resurrection, and He will make all things new. We believe this is true, and the rest of this guide asks you to weigh the evidence for it.

Why this matters

A single thread means each part of the Bible can be read in light of the whole. It also means the Bible's hope is not an idea but a promise with a plot: it names the problem (sin, evil and death), the rescuer (Jesus) and the ending (life with God), and it invites you to read the whole story for yourself.

Further watching: BibleProject (bibleproject.com) makes free animated videos that trace themes like this one, including heaven and earth, from Genesis to Revelation. They are a good way to see the thread for yourself.

Christ above all

Every thread of the Bible is gathered into one Person. God's plan is to unite all things in Christ, things in heaven and things on earth (Ephesians 1:10). Jesus is the center, the fulfillment and the Lord of the whole story.
