Foundations

One thread through 66 books

The Bible is 66 books by about 40 authors in three languages, written over roughly 1,500 years. Yet Jesus told two travelers that all of it, "Moses and all the Prophets", speaks of him (Luke 24:27). The thread that holds it together is hope: in a world that turned from God and is full of sin, suffering and death, God promises to rescue, and he keeps the promise.

Follow it through the library

The Law (Genesis to Deuteronomy)The promise comes at the darkest moment, right after the first rebellion: a descendant of the woman will crush the serpent (Genesis 3:15). God then promises Abraham that through his family all nations will be blessed (Genesis 12:3).
↓
The History (Joshua to Esther)Kings and people fail again and again. God promises David a descendant whose kingdom will last forever (2 Samuel 7:12-13).
↓
Wisdom and poetry (Job to Song of Solomon)Honest cries about suffering and longing for a rescuer: "I know that my Redeemer lives" (Job 19:25).
↓
The Prophets (Isaiah to Malachi)Hard warnings and great hope together: a child who will reign (Isaiah 9:6-7), a new heart for God's people (Ezekiel 36:26), and death swallowed up forever (Isaiah 25:8).
↓
The Gospels (Matthew to John)The promised rescuer arrives. Jesus dies for sin and rises, and the powers of darkness are defeated.
↓
Acts and the LettersWhat the rescue means: through Christ God reconciles "all things" to himself (Colossians 1:19-20), and he shares in our humanity "that through death he might bring to nothing him who had the power of death, that is, the devil" (Hebrews 2:14).
↓
RevelationThe promise is kept in full. Evil is finished, and God lives with his people.

Where the thread ends

The Bible ends with a final event that answers its first chapters. Sin is defeated, Satan is destroyed, death is destroyed, and God and his people live together in a renewed world. Notice how the last pages mirror the first.

The serpent
Genesis 1-3

Deceives humanity

Revelation 20-22

The devil is thrown down for good (Revelation 20:10)

Death
Genesis 1-3

Enters the world

Revelation 20-22

Death itself is destroyed (Revelation 20:14, 1 Corinthians 15:25-26)

God and people
Genesis 1-3

He walks with them in the garden, then they hide

Revelation 20-22

He lives with them and wipes away every tear (Revelation 21:3-4)

The tree of life
Genesis 1-3

The way is closed (Genesis 3:24)

Revelation 20-22

It stands open for all nations (Revelation 22:2)

The curse
Genesis 1-3

It falls on the ground and on people

Revelation 20-22

"There will be no curse any more" (Revelation 22:3)

Christians describe this as the gospel: the good news is not only that you can be forgiven, but that the whole world is being rescued and made new, and that you are invited to know God and live with him in it.

Why this matters

A single thread means each book can be read in light of the whole. It also means the Bible's hope is not an idea but a promise with a plot: it names the problem (sin, evil and death), the rescuer (Jesus) and the ending (life with God), and it invites you to read the whole story for yourself.
