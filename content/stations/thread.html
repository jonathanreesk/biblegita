One thread through the whole Bible

In "The Bible: one story of redemption (Mukti)" you walked through the Bible section by section. Here we follow one thread through all of it, and see where it ends. Jesus told two travelers that all of it, "Moses and all the Prophets", speaks of Him (Luke 24:27). The thread that holds it together is hope: in a world that turned from God and is full of sin, suffering and death, God promises redemption (mukti), and He keeps the promise.

Where the thread ends

The Bible ends with a final event that answers its first chapters. Sin is defeated, Satan is destroyed, death is destroyed, and God and His people live together in a renewed world. Notice how the last pages mirror the first.

The gospel

The good news is not only that you can be forgiven, but that the whole world is being redeemed and made new, and that you are invited to know God and live with Him in it.

The claim at the center

The Bible's answer to a world of sin, evil and death is a person. Jesus Christ has defeated sin, Satan and death by His cross and resurrection (Hebrews 2:14), and He will make all things new (Revelation 21:5). We believe this is true, and the rest of this guide asks you to weigh the evidence for it.

Why this matters

A single thread means each part of the Bible can be read in light of the whole. It also means the Bible's hope is not an idea but a promise with a plot: it names the problem (sin, evil and death), the Redeemer (Jesus) and the ending (life with God), and it invites you to read the whole story for yourself.

Further watching: BibleProject (bibleproject.com) makes free animated videos that trace themes like this one, including heaven and earth, from Genesis to Revelation. They are a good way to see the thread for yourself.

Christ above all

Every thread of the Bible is gathered into one Person. God's plan is to unite all things in Christ, things in heaven and things on earth (Ephesians 1:10). Jesus is the center, the fulfillment and the Lord of the whole story.
