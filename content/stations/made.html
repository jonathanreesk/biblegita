Made to know and be known

Beneath every philosophy sit a few simple questions. Is there someone behind all this? Does He know me? What am I here for? The Gita and the Bible both take these seriously. This guide is for any reader, whatever you grew up with, and you are welcome to ask honest questions all the way through.

What the Gita reaches for

That life is not an accident. That there is a Self worth knowing and a Lord worth loving, who says to Arjuna, "you are dear to Me" (Gita 18:65). Many Hindus know the joy of bhakti, devotion offered with love: "To those who are ever steadfast, worshipping me with love, I give the yoga of discrimination, by which they come to me" (Gita 10:10).

What the Bible says to that longing

That longing is not misplaced. It is what you were made for. The Bible's answer is not first about rules or rituals. It is about a God who is love, who made you to know Him, and who has come looking for you.

Three things the Bible says about you

1. You are loved before you do anything"God is love" (1 John 4:8). "We love him, because he first loved us" (1 John 4:19). He is pictured as a God who "will rejoice over you with joy" and "calm you in his love" (Zephaniah 3:17).
↓
2. You were made on purpose, women and men alike"In God's image he created him; male and female he created them" (Genesis 1:27). Both are given the same calling: to be fruitful, to care for the world, and to live together with God (Genesis 1:28, Genesis 2:15). When Jesus rose, He sent a woman, Mary Magdalene, to tell the others (John 20:16-18).
↓
3. You were made to know God, and joy is what that is like"This is eternal life, that they should know you, the only true God" (John 17:3). "In your presence there is fullness of joy" (Psalm 16:11). Jesus said, "that my joy may remain in you, and that your joy may be made full" (John 15:11).

Of Jesus, Peter wrote to people who had never seen Him: "Though you have not seen him, you love him ... and rejoice with joy that is inexpressible and filled with glory" (1 Peter 1:8).

What stands in the way

Something has gone wrong between us and the God who made us. The Bible does not describe this first as a broken rule but as a broken relationship: "your iniquities have separated you and your God" (Isaiah 59:2). Most people sense it: the gap between how we know we should live and how we do, between the love we want and the love we manage to give.

The good news is that God did not wait for us to fix it. "God commends his own love toward us, in that while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us" (Romans 5:8). Jesus invites: "Come to me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest" (Matthew 11:28).

God's answer is a person

Jesus is God come to us (John 1:14). He died to forgive our sin and rose from the dead (1 Corinthians 15:3-4). He says, "I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father, except through me" (John 14:6). We believe that, and we say it plainly. It is a large claim, and it is also an open door: the invitation is to everyone, from every background.

How to use this guide

The next steps compare the two scriptures honestly, one question at a time: how each is built, what each says about God, the self, justice and forgiveness, and how a person can be made right with God. Some of it asks hard questions of both books. A love worth having can bear honest questions.

The aim is not to win an argument. It is that you would know God and His love, find your place in what He is doing in the world, and find joy there.
