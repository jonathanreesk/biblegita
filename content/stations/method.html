Before you begin

How to walk this journey

This guide is written for a conversation between friends: usually a Christian and a Hindu, talking honestly about what each believes. The questions are about whether ideas hold together, never about mocking a text or the people who love it.

Reading as a Hindu, or as someone exploring? Wherever the guide says "your friend", read it as the Christian you are talking with. The questions are meant to run both ways.

  1. UnderstandListen until you can state their view in a way your friend would agree with.
  2. LocateFind the verse your friend has in mind. Look it up together; you do not need to know it by heart.
  3. TestWalk through the argument. Name the logical problem plainly.
  4. AnticipateKnow how a thoughtful Hindu would answer. If you cannot say what their best reply is, you have not yet understood their view.
  5. BridgeShow how the gospel meets the longing underneath the question.

How the four questions work

Later in the guide come four questions about the Hindu worldview. Each one is built the same way, so you always know where you are:

  • The Hindu claim: what the tradition teaches, stated fairly, with the verses.
  • The argument: a few short steps, each one following from the one before, that end in a conclusion. If you agree with every step, the conclusion follows. If you disagree, you can point to the exact step.
  • Expect this reply: the best answer a thoughtful Hindu would give.
  • Your strongest answer: how a Christian can respond to that reply.
  • Christ above all: where the question points, to Jesus.

Each question also names a logical issue, the kind of reasoning problem it is about, in plain words. You do not need to learn the names. They are there to help you see the shape of the problem.

One more thing to know: Hindu thought is not one system. Three main schools read the same texts differently: Advaita (all is one), Vishishtadvaita (God is personal, and we are part of Him) and Dvaita (God and souls are always distinct). Ask which one your friend holds before you press a point.

You do not need to know the Gita

This guide is not asking you to learn the Gita or the Mahabharata. Know the Bible, and know why you believe it. Where the Gita matters, this guide gives you the background you need, and the glossary and the verse cards are here to look things up.

Always be prepared to give an answer for the hope you have, with gentleness and respect. (1 Peter 3:15)

Christ above all

Every conversation has one aim: to honor Christ the Lord in our hearts and to lift Him up. He is the answer, not our arguments. "And I, when I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all people to myself" (John 12:32).
