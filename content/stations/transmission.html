Going deeper

How the texts were passed down

Hindu tradition divides scripture into Shruti ("what is heard": the Vedas, preserved orally with strict phonetic methods) and Smriti ("what is remembered": the epics, including the Gita, which grew through performance). The Bible was passed down through manuscript copying, then recognized as a closed canon.

What to draw out

The two traditions treat variation differently. For the epic, additions are enrichment. For the Bible, variants are errors to be corrected, because the claim is that God acted in history and the record of it matters. That is the apologist's real point: the question is not which text is older or longer, but which one claims to report events that can be checked.

Inerrancy and the manuscripts

Christians hold that the Bible, as God gave it, is true and without error in all that it affirms. This is called . It rests on what Scripture says about itself: it is "God-breathed" (2 Timothy 3:16), written by "holy men of God", who "spoke, being moved by the Holy Spirit" (2 Peter 1:21), and Jesus prayed, "Your word is truth" (John 17:17). The claim is about the original writings. So a fair question follows: how do we know the copies we hold today match them?

Codex Sinaiticus

One of the best answers sits in the British Library. Codex Sinaiticus is a Greek Bible copied by hand around AD 350, about 300 years after the apostles wrote, and it contains the complete New Testament along with much of the Old. It was found at St Catherine's Monastery on Mount Sinai, where it takes its name, and the surviving pages are now held in four countries and can be read page by page online at codexsinaiticus.org ↗.

Its value is that it can be compared. Set Sinaiticus beside Codex Vaticanus (about the same age) and the thousands of other Greek copies from different places and centuries, and the same text appears. The differences are mostly spelling and word order. Scribes also corrected it, and those corrections can still be seen on the pages.

An honest note

The copies are not identical. Sinaiticus leaves out the long ending of Mark (Mark 16:9-20) and the story of the woman caught in adultery in John 8, and good Bibles say so in a footnote. Careful readers weigh each difference. No teaching of the faith depends on a disputed passage.

Why it matters

Inerrancy is not a claim that every copyist was perfect. It is a claim about what God inspired, and the manuscripts let us check how closely we hold it. We can compare hundreds of independent witnesses, some within a few centuries of the events. A text that can be checked can be trusted.

Expect this reply

  • "The Bible has been changed so many times it cannot be trusted." Ask which part. The manuscripts show where the differences are, and they are small and openly recorded. Hindu tradition has also guarded its texts carefully by memory, so the real question is not care but checking: copies you can lay side by side and examine for yourself.
