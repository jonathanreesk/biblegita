The questions · Challenge 3

Punished for a life you cannot remember

Logical issue: personal identity (is the one who suffers the one who did wrong?)

The Hindu claim

The eternal self (atman) is never born and never dies. It moves from body to body like a person changing clothes, carrying the results of past action with it. The Upanishads teach this, and the Gita repeats it.

Gita 2:12Gita 2:20Gita 2:22

The apologetic argument

  1. Moral responsibility requires a continuing person who can know, own and repent of the act.
  2. Rebirth removes memory, personality and conscious continuity.
  3. Having the same soul is not the same as being the same person, one who remembers and can take responsibility.
  4. So karmic suffering punishes someone who is, in every way that matters morally, a different person.
Ezekiel 18:20Romans 2:6Hebrews 9:27

Expect this reply

  • "Karma is not punishment, it is consequence." Like gravity, it has no judge, so guilt is the wrong category.
  • "The subtle body carries tendencies (samskaras)." Character traits carry over even when memories do not.
  • "What about original sin?" Christians say we suffer for Adam, whom we cannot remember. Be ready for this one; it is the strongest reply.

Your strongest answer

On "consequence, not punishment": if karma is impersonal, it cannot be just or unjust, which undercuts its main appeal as a moral explanation of suffering. On original sin: The Bible distinguishes the sinful condition we inherit from the guilt of our own actions. We are judged for our own deeds, known to our own conscience (Romans 2:15), in one life we can remember. And where karma has no forgiveness, the gospel offers it: Christ bears the consequence Himself (Romans 5:8, 1 Peter 2:24).

Christ above all

You do not have to carry a debt you cannot remember or repay. Christ bore it Himself (1 Peter 2:24), and for those who are in Christ Jesus there is now no condemnation (Romans 8:1).
