The Mahabharata and the Bhagavad Gita

The Bhagavad Gita, often called simply the Gita, is not a book on its own. It is one conversation inside the Mahabharata, the longest Hindu epic, about a family at war. Before comparing ideas, know the plot: who is who, what happens, and whether anyone in it is truly good.

The Mahabharata: 18 parvas about a family war

Tradition credits the sage Vyasa with the Mahabharata, a poem of about 100,000 verses divided into 18 sections, each called a Parva in Sanskrit. It grew over several centuries and was told and retold by many people, mostly through spoken performance, so versions differ from region to region. What follows is the widely shared core of the story. Its central question is what dharma (duty) demands when duties collide.

Four moments of the war worth knowing

Four times, the side said to stand for dharma won by breaking a rule. Tap each. (Details differ a little between regional versions.)

Drona and "Ashwatthama is dead"

What happened. Drona, the Kauravas' commander, could not be beaten while armed. Bhima killed an elephant named Ashwatthama, the name of Drona's son, and shouted that Ashwatthama was dead. Drona would believe only Yudhishthira, who said it and added under his breath "man or elephant", while Krishna had the war conches blown to drown out the words. Drona laid down his weapons in grief and was killed.

Why it matters. Even the most truthful man told a half-truth to win. The epic says his chariot, which had floated above the ground, touched the earth from that day.

Karna and the stuck chariot

What happened. Karna's chariot wheel sank into the mud, and he asked Arjuna to wait, as the rules of war required. Krishna answered that Karna had broken those rules himself, when Draupadi was shamed and when young Abhimanyu was killed by many warriors at once. Arjuna shot, and Karna fell.

Why it matters. Can you claim the protection of the rules after breaking them? It is the moment Krishna's guidance looks most like "the end justifies the means".

Bhishma and the shield of Shikhandi

What happened. Bhishma could choose the hour of his own death, and had vowed never to fight a woman. Arjuna shot from behind Shikhandi, who had been born a woman, and Bhishma fell onto a bed of arrows.

Why it matters. The Pandavas used a loophole in the honor code of a man they loved, one Bhishma himself had told them. Even the noblest defeat in the epic is morally mixed.

Duryodhana struck below the belt

What happened. In a duel with maces, striking below the waist was forbidden. Krishna slapped his own thigh as a signal, and Bhima, remembering his vow over Draupadi, broke Duryodhana's thigh. Balarama, Krishna's own brother, who had taught Duryodhana, was furious at the foul blow.

Why it matters. A vow of revenge beat the rule of fair fighting, and even Krishna's brother called it wrong.

Taken together: the side said to stand for dharma won only by doing wrong, with Krishna's guidance. This is why many Hindus say dharma is "subtle": hard to see, and costly even when you see it.

Your strongest answer

A story that leaves good and evil unsettled cannot tell you which side you are on, or what to do about the wrong in your own heart. The Bible gives a clear standard and a Savior: Christ died "the righteous for the unrighteous, that he might bring us to God" (1 Peter 3:18).

How it ends

The war is only the middle of the Mahabharata. Its last books tell what happens after victory, and they are some of the darkest in the epic.

  1. After the warA hollow victory

    Yudhishthira rules for thirty-six years over a kingdom emptied by the war. Almost everyone the Pandavas loved is dead, including all of Draupadi's sons.

  2. Mausala ParvaKrishna's people destroy themselves

    As Gandhari's curse foretold, Krishna's clan turns on itself in a drunken brawl and is wiped out. Krishna is killed by a hunter's arrow, shot by mistake while he rests in the forest. His city, Dwaraka, sinks into the sea.

  3. Mahaprasthanika ParvaThe last walk

    The Pandavas give up the throne and walk toward the Himalayas to reach heaven. Draupadi falls and dies on the way, then the brothers one by one, each for a fault: pride, vanity, greed. Only Yudhishthira reaches the top, with a dog that has followed him.

  4. Svargarohana ParvaA confusing heaven

    In heaven Yudhishthira finds Duryodhana, the man who started the war, honored there, while his own brothers and Draupadi appear to be suffering in hell. It turns out to be an illusion, a test. In the end everyone, on both sides, is said to reach heaven. Then the dark age of Kali begins.

What it asks, and what the Bible announces

The Mahabharata asks

What is the right thing to do when every choice costs something? It never settles the answer, and the story ends in grief.

The Bible announces

What has God done about a broken world? Its answer is redemption (mukti), and the story ends in restoration.

Christ above all

The Mahabharata ends in grief. The Bible ends with Jesus Christ, the Lamb who was slain, honored by all creation (Revelation 5:12). He is the one who never fell short.
