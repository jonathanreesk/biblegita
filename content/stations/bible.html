The Bible: one story of redemption (Mukti)

You have seen the one thread. Now see how the whole Bible carries it. The Hindu texts are many voices that never agree. The Bible is one finished book that tells one story, from the first page to the last: man's sin and constant rebellion, and God's grace and mercy in His plan of salvation. The link between its two halves is Jesus.

The path of redemption (mukti)

  1. Law · Genesis to DeuteronomyRedemption promised

    ManTurns from God in the very first rebellion, and the world breaks.

    GodPromises a Redeemer who will crush the serpent (Genesis 3:15), redeems a nation from slavery, and shows what redemption costs in the blood of the Passover lamb (Exodus 12:13).

  2. History · Joshua to EstherRedemption needed

    ManPeople and kings rebel again and again. The problem is deeper than any leader.

    GodStays faithful, and promises David a descendant whose kingdom will last forever (2 Samuel 7:12-13).

  3. Poetry · Job to Song of SolomonRedemption longed for

    ManCries out in guilt, in grief and in doubt.

    GodAnswers with mercy: "as far as the east is from the west, so far does he remove our transgressions from us" (Psalm 103:12). "I know that my Redeemer lives" (Job 19:25).

  4. Prophets · Isaiah to MalachiRedemption foretold

    ManRebellion ends in exile, and the warnings go unheeded.

    GodPromises a servant "pierced for our transgressions" (Isaiah 53:5), a ruler from Bethlehem (Micah 5:2), and a new covenant: "I will forgive their iniquity" (Jeremiah 31:31-34).

  5. The link between the twoJesus

    Everything before Him points forward to Him. Jesus said "everything written about me in the Law of Moses and the Prophets and the Psalms must be fulfilled" (Luke 24:44). The Old Testament is the promise. The New Testament is the Promised One.

  6. Gospels · Matthew to JohnRedemption accomplished

    ManRejects Jesus and crucifies Him, the worst sin of all.

    GodGives Him as the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world (John 1:29). Jesus dies to forgive sin and rises from the dead. The cross is man's greatest sin and God's greatest grace.

  7. ActsRedemption proclaimed

    ManResists, and persecutes the first believers.

    GodSends the Holy Spirit, and the good news goes out from Jerusalem to every nation (Acts 1:8).

  8. Letters · Romans to JudeRedemption explained

    Man"All have sinned, and fall short of the glory of God" (Romans 3:23).

    God"justified by his grace as a gift, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus" (Romans 3:24).

  9. RevelationRedemption completed

    ManRebellion is finally ended and judged.

    GodDefeats sin and Satan and death, makes all things new, and lives with His people (Revelation 21:3-4).

What the one story says about you

  1. Loved before you do anything "We love because he first loved us" (1 John 4:19). He "will rejoice over you with gladness" (Zephaniah 3:17).
  2. Made on purpose Women and men alike: "in the image of God he created him; male and female he created them" (Genesis 1:27), with one calling (Genesis 1:28). The risen Jesus sent a woman, Mary Magdalene, to tell the others (John 20:16-18).
  3. Made to know God, with joy "this is eternal life, that they know you the only true God" (John 17:3). "in your presence there is fullness of joy" (Psalm 16:11).

"Though you have not seen him, you love him ... and rejoice with joy that is inexpressible and filled with glory" (1 Peter 1:8).

One problem, one solution, for all. From Genesis to Revelation the Bible names the same problem, sin, and the same answer, Christ, offered to "whoever believes" (John 3:16). The Hindu texts agree we are trapped in rebirth, but give five different reasons why (One problem, or many?).

Keep this in mind for every chapter that follows: the Bhagavad Gita is a conversation inside a battle. The gospel is not one conversation in the Bible. It is the plot of the whole Bible.

Christ above all

Every story ends somewhere. The Hindu texts end in an endless cycle or in the self dissolving away. The Bible ends with Jesus Christ, the Lamb who was slain, honored by all creation (Revelation 5:12). He is the point of the whole story.
