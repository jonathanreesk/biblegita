The questions · Challenge 4

Can every path be true?

Logical issue: law of non-contradiction

The Hindu claim

Truth is one, but the wise speak of it in many ways (Rig Veda 1.164.46). People come to God by different paths suited to their nature. In the Gita, Krishna says he receives people however they approach him.

Gita 4:11Gita 7:21Gita 9:23

The apologetic argument

  1. Religions make claims that directly contradict one another about the self, God and salvation.
  2. Two contradictory claims cannot both be true in the same sense.
  3. Saying they are all true "at the ultimate level" empties them of meaning.
  4. So pluralism either fails logically or quietly becomes one more exclusive view: that pluralism is right and everyone who disagrees is wrong.
John 14:6Acts 4:121 Corinthians 15:14-17

Expect this reply

  • "The blind men and the elephant." Each religion touches part of the truth.
  • "Logic is limited." Ultimate reality is beyond categories, so non-contradiction does not apply there.

Your strongest answer

The elephant story only works because the narrator sees the whole elephant, which is the very position the pluralist says no one has. "Logic does not apply to ultimate reality" is itself a claim that uses logic and expects to be believed over its opposite. Then point to the Gita itself: Krishna does not end with "all paths are equal" but calls Arjuna to abandon other duties and come to him alone (18:66). Even the Gita makes an exclusive call. The question is which exclusive call is true, and Christianity rests that answer on a checkable event, the resurrection. Jesus Christ is not one voice among many: He rose from the dead, and He alone says, "I am the way, the truth, and the life."

Christ above all

Jesus does not offer Himself as one path among many. He says, "I am the way" (John 14:6), and there is salvation in no one else (Acts 4:12). God has given Him the name above every name, and every knee will bow to Him (Philippians 2:9-11). He is Lord.
