The questions · Challenge 4

Can every path be true?

Logical issue: law of non-contradiction (two opposite claims cannot both be true)

The Hindu claim

Truth is one, but the wise speak of it in many ways (Rig Veda 1.164.46). People come to God by different paths suited to their nature. In the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna says he receives people however they approach him.

Gita 4:11Gita 7:21Gita 9:23

The apologetic argument

  1. Religions make claims that directly contradict one another about the self, God and salvation.
  2. Two contradictory claims cannot both be true in the same sense.
  3. Saying they are all true "at the ultimate level" empties them of meaning.
  4. So pluralism either fails logically or quietly becomes one more exclusive view: that pluralism is right and everyone who disagrees is wrong.

The story of the blind men and the elephant

An old Indian parable, told in Buddhist, Jain and Hindu traditions to explain why religions disagree.

Each is partly right, the story says, and no one has the whole truth. So, the claim goes, the religions describe parts of one God, and none can be the only true one.

Expect this reply

  • "The blind men and the elephant." Each religion touches part of the truth, like the men who each feel one part of the elephant (the story is told above).
  • "Logic is limited." Ultimate reality is beyond categories, so non-contradiction does not apply there.

Your strongest answer

  • Who tells the story? It only works because the narrator sees the whole elephant, the very view the pluralist says no one has.
  • "Logic does not apply" is itself a logical claim, and it expects to be believed over its opposite.
  • A few cases prove nothing. "All paths lead to God" is often argued from the experiences of people on different paths. However many experiences there are, they cannot prove a rule for everyone.
  • One counterexample is enough. "All paths lead to God" is a claim about every path. A single path that contradicts the others breaks it, the same way one black swan breaks "all swans are white" (see the two tests in Before you begin).
  • Even the Gita ends with one call: Krishna tells Arjuna to abandon other duties and come to him alone (Gita 18:66).
  • So which call is true? Christianity rests its answer on an event that can be checked: the resurrection. Jesus rose from the dead, and He alone says, "I am the way, and the truth, and the life."
Christ above all

Jesus does not offer Himself as one path among many. He says, "I am the way" (John 14:6), and there is salvation in no one else (Acts 4:12). God has given Him the name above every name, and every knee will bow to Him (Philippians 2:9-11). He is Lord.
