Foundations

Preserved, but who can understand?

A scripture can be kept perfectly and still be out of reach. Two practical questions are worth asking of any sacred library: what shape is it, and who is it for? Hindu tradition has preserved its texts with remarkable care. The Bible makes a different claim about itself.

Shape of the library
Hindu scriptures

Many streamsVedas, Upanishads, epics, Puranas and schools (Advaita, Dvaita and others) that read them differently. There is no single plot.

The Bible

One storylineMany books that follow one rescue from creation to new creation (Luke 24:27).

The shape of time
Hindu scriptures

A cycleAges (yugas) that repeat without end.

The Bible

A lineA beginning, a turning point at the cross and an end.

Where it is heading
Hindu scriptures

Release of the soulMoksha: the individual is freed from rebirth while the world keeps turning.

The Bible

Renewal of everythingSin, Satan and death defeated, and God dwelling with his people.

How it was kept
Hindu scriptures

Memorized with great precisionTrained reciters preserved the sounds of the Vedas for thousands of years. Meaning passed through teachers and commentaries.

The Bible

Copied and comparedThousands of manuscripts, then translations and print, so anyone can check the text.

Who it was meant for
Hindu scriptures

Mostly specialistsOrthodox tradition reserved Vedic study for initiated men of the upper classes, in Sanskrit. Many modern teachers reject that limit.

The Bible

EveryoneWritten in the common languages of its day and meant to be understood by all.

A fair picture

What the Hindu tradition offers the ordinary person

The Gita itself opens the path wide: those "of a sinful birth, women, Vaisyas and Sudras" can all reach the highest goal by taking refuge in God (Gita 9:32). The epics are told in villages, bhakti poets such as Tulsidas and Kabir wrote in everyday languages, and temple worship is open to many. Hindus often say that many paths to the truth are the point, not a problem.

What the Bible claims about itself

"The entrance of your words gives light. It gives understanding to the simple" (Psalm 119:130). When the law was read to the whole people, men, women and children, the teachers "gave the sense, so that they understood the reading" (Nehemiah 8:8). At Pentecost, people from many nations heard the good news "in our own native language" (Acts 2:8).

Expect this reply

  • "Many voices are a strength." Diversity can be a strength, and many Hindus value it. Ask a gentle question: when someone sincerely asks which teaching is the true one, how does a reader decide? The Bible answers its own question with one story that ends in one hope.
  • "The Bible has many versions and denominations too." It does. The difference to explore is whether the library has a single center. Jesus says it does: all of it speaks of him.

The Bible's claim is that God wants to be known by everyone, so he spoke in ordinary languages, to ordinary people, in a story anyone can follow.
