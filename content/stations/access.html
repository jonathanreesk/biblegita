Can the texts be trusted?

Preserved, but who can understand?

A sacred text can be kept perfectly and still be out of reach. Two practical questions are worth asking of any sacred text: what shape is it, and who is it for? Hindu tradition has preserved its texts with remarkable care. The Bible makes a different claim about itself.

Shape of the text
Hindu texts

Many separate texts and schoolsVedas, Upanishads, epics, Puranas and schools (Advaita, Dvaita and others) that read them differently. There is no single plot.

The Bible

One finished BibleA single compiled text that follows one rescue from creation to new creation (Luke 24:27).

How it was kept
Hindu texts

Memorized with great precisionTrained reciters preserved the sounds of the Vedas for thousands of years. Meaning passed through teachers and commentaries.

The Bible

Copied and comparedThousands of manuscripts, then translations and print, so anyone can check the text.

Who it was meant for
Hindu texts

Mostly specialistsOrthodox tradition reserved Vedic study for initiated men of the upper classes, in Sanskrit. Many modern teachers reject that limit.

The Bible

EveryoneWritten in the common languages of its day and meant to be understood by all.

A fair picture

What the Hindu tradition offers the ordinary person

The Gita itself opens the path wide: those "of a sinful birth, women, Vaisyas and Sudras" can all reach the highest goal by taking refuge in God (Gita 9:32). The epics are told in villages, bhakti poets such as Tulsidas and Kabir wrote in everyday languages, and temple worship is open to many. Hindus often say that many paths to the truth are the point, not a problem.

The Bible's answer: the Word became a person

Where does Christian faith find understanding of God? Not in a technique or a trained class, but in a person. "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God" (John 1:1), and "The Word became flesh, and lived among us" (John 1:14). God's message did not stay locked in a text for specialists. It came to us as someone who could be met, heard and followed. Jesus says, "I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father, except through me" (John 14:6).

The written Word serves the same purpose. It "gives understanding to the simple" (Psalm 119:130). It was read aloud and explained to men, women and children, so that "they understood the reading" (Nehemiah 8:8), and at Pentecost it was heard "in our own native language" (Acts 2:8).

Expect this reply

  • "Many voices are a strength." Diversity can be a strength, and many Hindus value it. Ask a gentle question: when someone sincerely asks which teaching is the true one, how does a reader decide? The Bible answers its own question with one story that ends in one hope.
  • "The Bible has many versions and denominations too." It does. The difference to explore is whether the Bible has a single center. Jesus says it does: all of it speaks of Him.

The Bible's claim is that God wants to be known by everyone, so He spoke in ordinary languages, to ordinary people, in a story anyone can follow.

Christ above all

The Word who became flesh is open to every person. "Come to me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest" (Matthew 11:28). Whoever comes to Him He will never cast out (John 6:37).
