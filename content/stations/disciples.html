Go and make disciples

Matthew 28:18-20
Why this is good news, not pressure

Making disciples is not recruiting for a group. It is inviting others into what you have found: knowing God, being loved by him, and living in his purposes with joy (John 15:11). It begins with listening, because each person is loved by God as they are, before they believe anything.

Jesus did not send us to win debates or collect decisions. He sent us to make disciples, baptize them and teach them to obey, with his authority and his presence. The arguments in this guide open doors; discipleship walks a person through them.

A pathway for a Hindu friend
  1. Befriend and prayEat together, honor their family, pray for them by name. Look for the person of peace (Luke 10:5-7).
  2. Discover ScriptureRead Luke or John together. Ask: what does this show about God, about people, what will you do?
  3. Meet JesusPresent the cross and empty tomb. Invite repentance and faith, turning from idols to serve the living God (1 Thess 1:9).
  4. BaptismA public step of belonging to Christ. Prepare them for its cost at home and in law.
  5. Teach to obeyPrayer, the Word, fellowship, the Lord's Supper, and new habits at home (Acts 2:42).
  6. MultiplyHelp them reach their own household and train others who will teach others (Acts 16:31, 2 Tim 2:2).

Know the law where you serve

Maharashtra passed the Freedom of Religion Act in March 2026, joining other Indian states with similar laws. A person who intends to change religion must give the authorities 60 days' notice, and conversions involving force, fraud or inducement carry serious penalties. Before any baptism, make sure the new believer understands the requirement, take advice from a lawyer or your church leadership, and never offer money, jobs or help as a reason to believe. Faith must be freely chosen.

The Lord's goal is a people from every nation, tribe and language, standing before the throne and the Lamb (Revelation 7:9). Every Hindu friend you pray for is someone he wants in that crowd.
