Can the texts be trusted?

How the texts are built

We have seen what the texts teach. Now the question underneath everything: can they be trusted? Start with where each one comes from: who wrote it, when, and whether it rests on events anyone could check.

Where do they come from?

Before comparing what the texts say, ask where each one comes from. Here the two are very different, and the difference counts against the Hindu texts as historical records.

Who wrote it?
Hindu texts

No known authorThe Vedas are said to be apaurusheya, "not made by any person": eternal sounds that sages "heard". The Mahabharata is credited to Vyasa, a name that means "compiler", but scholars find many hands over centuries.

The Bible

Named authorsAbout forty writers over about 1,500 years, many named: Moses, David, Solomon, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Daniel, Matthew, Mark, Luke, John, Paul, Peter, James and Jude, writing in known places. Luke says he investigated everything and wrote it down in order (Luke 1:1-4), and John writes of what "touched with our hands" (1 John 1:1).

When?
Hindu texts

Unknown and disputedScholars date the Vedas to roughly 1500 to 1000 BCE and the Upanishads to about 800 to 500 BCE, but tradition says the Vedas have no beginning. The Mahabharata grew across about 800 years. The date the Gita entered the Mahabharata is debated.

The Bible

A known spanAbout 1,500 years, tied to kings, empires and cities that can be checked against other records.

Is it one text?
Hindu texts

No single text, no closed listIt was never gathered into one volume, and no council or moment fixed which texts count. Beyond the four Vedas, communities and sects disagree about which texts carry authority.

The Bible

One Bible, one canonThe writings were collected and recognized by the community that received them, and the finished text is closed.

Is it tied to real events?
Hindu texts

Authority rests on teachingThe Gita's authority comes from what it says, not from a public event you can verify. The great war cannot be dated or located with confidence.

The Bible

Authority rests on eventsThe exodus, the exile and above all the life, death and resurrection of Jesus, with eyewitness claims (1 John 1:1-3).

Expect this reply

  • "That is the point: the truth is eternal and does not depend on history or on any person." Many Hindus hold this sincerely.

Your strongest answer

A text with no author, no date and no fixed form cannot be checked. You cannot ask who wrote it, when, or whether the writer was there. The Bible makes claims that can be tested: it names its witnesses and rests everything on one public event. "if Christ has not been raised, then our preaching is in vain and your faith is in vain" (1 Corinthians 15:14). A claim that can be tested can also be confirmed.

Size is not the point: the Bible is far shorter than the Hindu texts put together, yet it tells one story from beginning to end.

The honest answer: the core (the Gospels, Paul, the Torah, the Prophets) is shared by every Christian tradition.

Christ above all

The Bible is not about many things. It is about one Person. Jesus said the Scriptures, which we now hold as the Bible, bear witness about Him (John 5:39), and He is the image of the invisible God (Colossians 1:15).
