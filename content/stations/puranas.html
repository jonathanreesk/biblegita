The two stories

The Puranas and the gods

The Puranas are the stories behind most Hindu worship today: the gods, their families, the ages of the world and the pilgrimage places. They are also where the Hindu texts contradict each other most openly.

What they are

There are eighteen major Puranas, plus many smaller ones. Tradition says each Purana should cover five things: the creation of the world, its repeated re-creation, the families of gods and sages, the ages of the world, and the histories of the royal lines.

  • Vishnu-focused Puranas, such as the Vishnu Purana and the Bhagavata Purana, which tells the story of Krishna at length and is the main text of Krishna devotion.
  • Shiva-focused Puranas, such as the Shiva Purana and the Linga Purana.
  • Devi (goddess) texts, such as the Devi Mahatmya, which praise the Mother Goddess as the highest power.

Theme and framework

The problem

Disorder in the world and bondage to the cycle of rebirth.

The way out

Devotion (bhakti) to a chosen god, with worship, pilgrimage and the god's grace.

The goal

Life with the god, or release from rebirth. Each tradition names its own god as supreme.

This is where the idea of the cycles of ages, ending with the dark age of Kali, is spelled out. It is also where grace and personal devotion become the centre of Hindu life. Many Hindus find it closer to the Gita's teaching of bhakti than to the Vedic rituals.

The gods

Hindu texts speak about many gods, and the picture changes across the layers.

  • In the Vedas the main gods are forces of nature and order, such as Indra (storm and king of gods), Agni (fire), Varuna (cosmic order) and Surya (sun).
  • In the later texts three gods stand out: Brahma the creator, Vishnu the preserver and Shiva the destroyer and renewer. Many Hindus worship one of them above the others.
  • Avatars. An avatar is a god coming down to earth in a form. Vishnu is said to have come many times, in ten classic forms. The best known are Rama and Krishna.
  • One or many? Many Hindus say the many gods are different faces of the one reality, Brahman. Others hold that one Lord, such as Vishnu or Shiva, is supreme. There is no single Hindu answer.

Put to the test

Who is the highest God?
The Puranas

It depends which one you readIn the Vishnu Puranas, Brahma is born from a lotus in Vishnu's navel (Bhagavata Purana 3.8). In the Shiva Puranas, Vishnu and Brahma search for the end of Shiva's pillar of light and cannot find it (Shiva Purana). In the Goddess texts, the Mother is above all three. Each makes its own god supreme. They cannot all be true.

The Bible

One God, and no rival"I am Yahweh, and there is no one else. Besides me, there is no God" (Isaiah 45:5).

Are the gods good?
The Puranas

Not reliablyThe stories tell of gods who lie, lust, rage and deceive. Indra seduces the wife of the sage Gautama (Ramayana, Bala Kanda). Brahma desires the woman he himself created (Matsya Purana). Many Hindus read these as symbols, but the stories still teach that the gods are like us.

The Bible

Good all the way through"God is light, and in him is no darkness at all" (1 John 1:5). Jesus is that goodness in a human life, without one sin (Hebrews 4:15).

Did it happen?
The Puranas

No one claims witnessesAges lasting millions of years, gods with many heads and arms, and the same story told differently in each Purana. They do not claim to report events anyone saw.

The Bible

Done in the openPaul told a king that these things were not "done in a corner" (Acts 26:26). God acted in history, before witnesses.

What happens to sin?
The Puranas

It is washed or chanted awayBathe in the Ganges, repeat a god's name, or go on pilgrimage, and sins are said to be removed. Nothing is said about justice: the wrong is simply cancelled.

The Bible

It is paid for"Apart from shedding of blood there is no remission" (Hebrews 9:22). Sin is not ignored. Jesus paid it Himself, so God is both just and the one who forgives (Romans 3:26).

Avatars, such as Rama and Krishna, are not like Jesus. The full comparison is in "The Ramayana".

Expect this reply

  • "These are symbols and lessons, not history or rules." Many Hindus read the stories this way, and say that being good matters more than the details of a story.

Your strongest answer

Then ask, gently: if the stories are only symbols, how do you know which parts to keep and which to leave? Where does the standard of "good" come from? The Bible answers that the standard is not a story but a person. God Himself is good, and He showed it fully in Jesus, who never fell short.

What they ask, and what the Bible announces

The Puranas asks

Which god will you serve, and how will you win his favor? Each Purana gives a different god and a different answer.

The Bible announces

There is one God, and He came to serve you: "the Son of Man came not to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many" (Mark 10:45).

Christ above all

The gods of the Puranas come down to fight, to play and to be worshipped. Jesus came down to die. He "emptied himself, taking the form of a servant ... becoming obedient to the point of death, yes, the death of the cross" (Philippians 2:7-8). No god of the Puranas did that, and that is why every knee will bow to Him (Philippians 2:10-11).
