The Hindu texts in depth

The Puranas and the gods

The Puranas are the library of stories that shaped Hindu worship as it is practised today: the gods, their families, the ages of the world and the holy places.

Do they fit inside the Mahabharata?

No. The Puranas are later, separate texts, mostly dated to about 300 to 1000 CE. They are in the same family as the epics, and tradition even says the sage Vyasa, who is credited with the Mahabharata, also compiled the Puranas. The closest link is the Harivamsa, a later appendix to the Mahabharata that tells the life of Krishna and his family. It is the bridge between the epic and the Puranas.

What they are

There are eighteen major Puranas, plus many smaller ones. Tradition says each Purana should cover five things: the creation of the world, its repeated re-creation, the families of gods and sages, the ages of the world, and the histories of the royal lines.

  • Vishnu-focused Puranas, such as the Vishnu Purana and the Bhagavata Purana, which tells the story of Krishna in loving detail and is the most loved text of Krishna devotion.
  • Shiva-focused Puranas, such as the Shiva Purana and the Linga Purana.
  • Devi (goddess) texts, such as the Devi Mahatmya, which praise the Mother Goddess as the highest power.

Theme and framework

The problem

Disorder in the world and bondage to the cycle of rebirth.

The way out

Devotion (bhakti) to a chosen god, with worship, pilgrimage and the god's grace.

The goal

Life with the god, or release from rebirth. Each tradition names its own god as supreme.

This is where the idea of the cycles of ages, ending with the dark age of Kali, is spelled out. It is also where grace and personal devotion become the centre of Hindu life. Many Hindus find it closer to the Gita's teaching of bhakti than to the Vedic rituals.

The gods

Hindu texts speak about many gods, and the picture changes across the layers.

  • In the Vedas the main gods are forces of nature and order, such as Indra (storm and king of gods), Agni (fire), Varuna (cosmic order) and Surya (sun).
  • In the later texts three gods stand out: Brahma the creator, Vishnu the preserver and Shiva the destroyer and renewer. Many Hindus worship one of them above the others.
  • Avatars. An avatar is a god coming down to earth in a form. Vishnu is said to have come many times, in ten classic forms. The best known are Rama and Krishna.
  • One or many? Many Hindus say the many gods are different faces of the one reality, Brahman. Others hold that one Lord, such as Vishnu or Shiva, is supreme. There is no single Hindu answer.

Good and bad in the gods

In many of the stories the gods show anger, jealousy, pride and desire, as people do. Most Hindus do not read this as a problem. They read the stories as lessons: take what is good, and leave the rest. It is the same way many read the Mahabharata, where no one is fully good and no one fully bad.

The Hindu reading

The gods and the heroes are mixed. Pick up the good, ignore the bad, and learn from what happens when people do each.

What the Bible says of God

God is good all the way through: "God is light, and in him is no darkness at all" (1 John 1:5). And the line is clear: "He who has the Son has the life. He who doesn't have God's Son doesn't have the life" (1 John 5:12).

Expect this reply

  • "These are symbols and lessons, not rules or history." Many Hindus read the stories this way, and say that being good is more important than the details of a story.

Your strongest answer

Then ask, gently: how does a person know which parts to take and which parts to leave? Where does the standard of "good" come from? The Bible answers that the standard is not a story but a person. God Himself is good, and He showed it fully in Jesus, who never fell short.

Christ above all

The Puranas picture gods who come down, who love their worshippers and who rescue. The Bible says God really did come, once, as a man, and went all the way to the cross: He "emptied himself, taking the form of a servant ... becoming obedient to the point of death, yes, the death of the cross" (Philippians 2:7-8). The God who came is wholly good, and He gave His life for us (John 10:11).
