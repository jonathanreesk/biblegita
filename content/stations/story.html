The two stories

Two big stories

Before comparing ideas, know the plot. The Mahabharata is an epic about a family at war. The Bible is one story about the Creator and the world He made, told in one finished Bible. The Gita is a pause inside one battle. The gospel is the turning point of the whole Bible.

The Mahabharata: 18 parvas about a family war

Tradition credits the sage Vyasa with the Mahabharata, a poem of about 100,000 verses divided into 18 sections, each called a Parva in Sanskrit. It grew over several centuries. Its central question is what dharma (duty) demands when duties collide.

Who is who: two sides of one family

Both sides are cousins from the same royal family. This is not one people fighting outsiders. It is a family tearing itself apart, and nearly every name below is related to another.

The Pandavas

The five sons of the late king Pandu.

  • Yudhishthira The eldest, known for truthfulness. He gambles the kingdom away.
  • Bhima The second, the strongest.
  • Arjuna The third, the finest archer. He is the one who speaks with Krishna in the Gita.
  • Nakula and Sahadeva The twins, the youngest two.
  • Draupadi Their wife, shamed in open court.
  • Kunti Their mother.
The Kauravas

The hundred sons of the blind king Dhritarashtra, who hold the throne.

  • Duryodhana The eldest, who refuses to share the kingdom.
  • Dushasana His brother, who drags Draupadi into the court.
  • Dhritarashtra Their blind father, the king.
  • Gandhari Their mother, who lost all her sons and curses Krishna.
  • Shakuni Their uncle, who tricks Yudhishthira at dice.
Krishna: neither side

Krishna is not a Pandava or a Kaurava. He is the Pandavas' cousin and Arjuna's close friend. Before the war he offered his great army to one side and himself, without weapons, to the other. Duryodhana chose the army. Arjuna chose Krishna. So Krishna fights for no one: he drives Arjuna's chariot and speaks the Gita. Many Hindus worship him as God.

Fighting for the Kauravas, out of loyalty
  • Bhishma The grand elder of the family, bound by a vow to defend the throne.
  • Drona The teacher who trained both the Pandavas and the Kauravas.
  • Karna A great archer who is secretly the Pandavas' eldest brother, born to Kunti before her marriage. He fights for Duryodhana, his only friend.
The tellers of the story
  • Vyasa The poet credited with the Mahabharata. He is also the grandfather of both families.
  • Sanjaya The king's minister, who tells Dhritarashtra what happens on the battlefield, including the Gita.
1. A family quarrelTwo sets of cousins, the five Pandavas and the hundred Kauravas, both claim the throne of Hastinapura.
↓
2. The dice gameYudhishthira, eldest of the Pandavas, gambles away his kingdom, his brothers and his wife Draupadi, who is shamed in open court. The Pandavas go into exile for 13 years.
↓
3. Peace failsThe Kauravas refuse to return even five villages. Krishna goes as an envoy and is turned away.
↓
4. The eve of battle (Parva 6)On the field of Kurukshetra, Arjuna sees teachers and relatives on the other side and puts down his bow. Krishna, driving his chariot, answers him: this is the Gita.
↓
5. Eighteen days of warAlmost everyone on both sides dies. The Pandavas win, and rule over a ruined kingdom.
↓
6. The endingKrishna's own clan destroys itself. The Pandavas set out on a last journey, and only Yudhishthira reaches heaven.

How the Gita fits: the whole epic is being told to the blind king Dhritarashtra by his minister Sanjaya, which is why the Gita begins with the king asking what his sons did on the battlefield. Scholars debate when the Gita was placed into the Mahabharata, but within the story it is one conversation, held just before the fighting begins.

The Bible: one story in six acts

The Bible is one finished text, compiled from 66 writings in the Protestant canon (39 in the Old Testament, 27 in the New), written by about 40 authors over roughly 1,500 years. The Mahabharata follows one royal family and its war. The Bible follows something larger: God's one plan to rescue the whole world, from the first page to the last.

1. CreationGod makes a good world and people to know Him and care for it (Genesis 1:1).

6. New creationChrist returns, evil is ended, and God lives with His people (Revelation 21:1-3).

The Mahabharata asks

What is the right thing to do when every choice costs something? Its answer is wrestled over, never settled, and the story ends in loss.

The Bible announces

What has God done about a broken world? Its answer is a rescue, and the story ends in restoration.

Keep this in mind for every step that follows: the Gita is a conversation inside a battle. The gospel is not one conversation in the Bible. It is the plot of the whole Bible.

Christ above all

Every story ends somewhere. The Mahabharata ends in loss and a lonely climb. The Bible ends with Jesus Christ, the Lamb who was slain, honored by all creation (Revelation 5:12). He is the point of the whole story.
