Foundations

Two big stories

Before comparing ideas, know the plot. The Mahabharata is an epic about a family at war. The Bible is one story about the Creator and the world He made, told across 66 books. The Gita is a pause inside one battle. The gospel is the turning point of the whole Bible.

The Mahabharata: 18 books about a family war

Tradition credits the sage Vyasa with the Mahabharata, a poem of about 100,000 verses divided into 18 books, each called a Parva in Sanskrit. It grew over several centuries. Its central question is what dharma (duty) demands when duties collide.

1. A family quarrelTwo sets of cousins, the five Pandavas and the hundred Kauravas, both claim the throne of Hastinapura.
↓
2. The dice gameYudhishthira, eldest of the Pandavas, gambles away his kingdom, his brothers and his wife Draupadi, who is shamed in open court. The Pandavas go into exile for 13 years.
↓
3. Peace failsThe Kauravas refuse to return even five villages. Krishna goes as an envoy and is turned away.
↓
4. The eve of battle (Book 6)On the field of Kurukshetra, Arjuna sees teachers and relatives on the other side and puts down his bow. Krishna, driving his chariot, answers him: this is the Gita.
↓
5. Eighteen days of warAlmost everyone on both sides dies. The Pandavas win, and rule over a ruined kingdom.
↓
6. The endingKrishna's own clan destroys itself. The Pandavas set out on a last journey, and only Yudhishthira reaches heaven.

How the Gita fits: the whole epic is being told to the blind king Dhritarashtra by his minister Sanjaya, which is why the Gita begins with the king asking what his sons did on the battlefield. Scholars debate when the Gita was placed into the epic, but within the story it is one conversation, held just before the fighting begins.

The Bible: one story in six acts

The Bible holds 66 books in the Protestant canon (39 in the Old Testament, 27 in the New), written by about 40 authors over roughly 1,500 years. Unlike an epic that follows one family, it follows one rescue plan from the first page to the last.

1. CreationGod makes a good world and people to know Him and care for it (Genesis 1:1).
↓
2. The FallPeople turn from God. Sin, death and broken relationships enter the world (Romans 5:12).
↓
3. PromiseGod calls Abraham to bless every nation (Genesis 12:2-3), rescues Israel from slavery in Egypt (Exodus 20:2), and sends prophets who promise a new covenant (Jeremiah 31:31).
↓
4. ChristGod Himself comes. Jesus lives, dies to forgive sin and rises again (Galatians 4:4, 1 Corinthians 15:3-4).
↓
5. The churchThe good news spreads from Jerusalem to every nation (Acts 1:8).
↓
6. New creationChrist returns, evil is ended, and God lives with His people (Revelation 21:1-3).

The Mahabharata asks

What is the right thing to do when every choice costs something? Its answer is wrestled over, never settled, and the story ends in loss.

The Bible announces

What has God done about a broken world? Its answer is a rescue, and the story ends in restoration.

Keep this in mind for every step that follows: the Gita is a conversation inside a battle. The gospel is not one conversation in the Bible. It is the plot of the whole book.
