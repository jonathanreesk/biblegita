The problem first

Anyone who fixes a complex system starts the same way: say clearly what is wrong, agree on it, find what fixes it, and make sure the fix works for every case. We can ask the same of any text that claims to speak for God.

  1. State the problemSay clearly what is wrong.
  2. Agree on itOne problem, named the same way throughout.
  3. Find the solutionSomething that actually fixes that problem.
  4. Apply it to allIt works for every person, not only some.

Step 1: what is the problem?

The Hindu texts each name a problem, but they do not agree on what it is. Each problem leads to a different way out.

  • 1VedasBeing out of step with the gods and the order of the world. Way out: the right ritual.
  • 2UpanishadsIgnorance: not knowing who you really are. Way out: knowledge.
  • 3Bhagavad GitaBondage to action and rebirth, "a place of pain and impermanence" (Gita 8:15). Way out: action, knowledge or devotion.
  • 4RamayanaEvil and disorder, in the form of Ravana. Way out: an avatar who defeats it.
  • 5PuranasDisorder and rebirth, with each god named supreme. Way out: devotion to your chosen god.
  • 1The BibleOne problem, from the first pages to the last: sin, which separates us from God. "Your iniquities have made a separation between you and your God" (Isaiah 59:2). "All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God" (Romans 3:23).
The Bible: one problem, one solution, for allSin entered the world through one man, and death through sin (Romans 5:12). God's answer is one person.
  1. The problemSin separates every person from a holy God.
  2. The solutionChrist, "who gave himself as a ransom for all" (1 Timothy 2:5-6).
  3. For whom"Whoever believes in him" (John 3:16): every person, from every background.

How truth travels

A second rule of clear thinking: some claims are about everyone (universals), and some are about one case (particulars). Truth moves between them in only certain directions.

↓Universal to particular: always holdsIf all dogs bark, this dog barks. If something is true of everyone, it is true of you.
↑Particular to universal: cannot prove itOne brown dog does not mean all dogs are brown. A few cases cannot prove a rule for everyone.
✕One counterexample breaks a universal"All swans are white" is false the moment one black swan is found.

Put to the test

The Hindu texts

  • Bottom up. "Truth is one; the wise call it by many names" (Rig Veda 1.164.46) moves from many people's experiences to a rule for all. Many cases cannot prove a universal.
  • Broken by one case. "All paths lead to God" is a universal. One path that contradicts the others is enough to break it, as Can every path be true? shows.

The Bible

  • Top down. "All have sinned" includes you. "Whoever believes" includes you too. The universal reaches every particular person.
  • Open to one test. The Bible rests everything on one event that could be checked: "if Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile and you are still in your sins" (1 Corinthians 15:17). It names the counterexample that would break it, and invites you to look.

Keep these two questions in mind on every step that follows: what problem does this text name, and does its answer hold for everyone?

Christ above all

The Bible names one problem for every person, and gives one answer for every person: "there is one God, and there is one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus" (1 Timothy 2:5).
