The two stories

All the Hindu texts, and how they fit

The Bible is one book with one story. Hindu tradition has many texts, written over about 3,000 years, with no single story and no closed list. This step is the map: what the texts are and how they fit together. The next steps take each group in turn and show where it goes wrong. Then comes the Bible's one story, and the two side by side.

The map

Hindus sort their texts into Shruti, "what was heard", the oldest and most respected, and Smriti, "what was remembered", which came later. A third group came later still: teachers explaining the older texts, manuals for temple worship, and devotional songs. Most Hindus do not rank these as Shruti or Smriti, but they shape how Hindus think and worship every day.

Only the Gita and a few teaching sections are part of the Mahabharata. The other texts on the map are separate works.

Ranked highest is not the same as read most

  • Ranked highest: the Vedas. On paper they have the final word, and when texts disagree, Shruti is meant to win. In practice they are mostly chanted in ritual, in Sanskrit, and rarely studied by ordinary Hindus.
  • Read and quoted most: the Gita. It ranks second, as Smriti, yet it is the text Hindus turn to for teaching, which is why this guide focuses on it.
  • Shapes daily worship most: the Puranas, the epics and devotional songs. These are the stories told at home, the festivals and the temple gods.

So there is no single Hindu answer to which text decides a question. The Bible has no such gap: the book that has the final word is the same book every Christian is meant to read, "profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction" (2 Timothy 3:16).

In time order

The dates below are scholars' estimates. Tradition places most of these texts far earlier.

  1. About 1500 to 1000 BCEThe Vedas

    Hymns and ritual instructions for the sacrifice.

  2. About 800 to 500 BCEThe Upanishads

    The philosophy of Brahman and Atman, and the first clear teaching of karma and rebirth.

  3. About 500 BCE to 400 CEThe Ramayana and the Mahabharata

    The two Hindu epics. Many hands added to them over centuries, and no one knows their first form. The Gita is inside the Mahabharata.

  4. About 200 BCE to 400 CELaw books and the Sutras

    The Manusmriti and other law books, the Brahma Sutras and the Yoga Sutras.

  5. About 300 to 1000 CEThe Puranas and temple manuals

    Stories of the gods, and the manuals for temple worship. The gods and the worship known today take their shape here.

  6. About 600 to 1700 CEDevotional poetry

    Poet-saints writing in everyday languages, from the Tamil Alvars to Tulsidas, Jnaneshwar and Tukaram.

Three roads, and where each one fails

Hindus say the texts offer three roads to the same goal, release from the cycle of rebirth (moksha). But the roads do not even agree on what is wrong with us, and not one of them deals with sin.

The way of action, from the Vedas
What it says

Do your duty and the rituals correctlyThe problem is being out of step with the gods and the order of the world. Keep dharma and offer the right sacrifices.

Where it fails

No one keeps it, and ritual cannot remove guiltNo one does their duty perfectly, and the Bible says plainly that "it is impossible that the blood of bulls and goats should take away sins" (Hebrews 10:4).

The way of knowledge, from the Upanishads
What it says

Realize that your true self is BrahmanThe problem is ignorance of who you really are. Knowledge sets you free.

Where it fails

It calls sin ignorance, and erases the personWrongdoing is treated as a misunderstanding, not as guilt before a holy God. And the goal is for the self to dissolve into Brahman, so there is no one left to be loved or forgiven.

The way of devotion, from the Gita, the epics and the Puranas
What it says

Love and surrender to a personal LordThe problem is bondage to rebirth. Worship your chosen god, and he will lift you out.

Where it fails

Which god, and what about justice?Each tradition names a different god as supreme, and the Gita's Krishna wins his war by deceit. None of them says how sin is paid for. They only promise that it will be overlooked.

The Bible: not a road up, but God come down

Every one of the three roads depends on you: your duty, your insight, your devotion. The Bible names one problem, sin against a holy God, and one answer, which you cannot earn: "by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works" (Ephesians 2:8-9). Jesus did not show us a road. He said, "I am the way" (John 14:6).

The Gita tries to hold all three roads together in one conversation, which is why its own commentators still disagree about which one comes first. The Upanishads are also the source of the ideas tested later in "Monism and the personal God", "Karma and the endless chain" and "Punished for a life you cannot remember".

Christ above all

Among all the ways people have pictured God, one is different. God did not stay in a story or a symbol: He became a person, "full of grace and truth" (John 1:14), and He is good all the way through.
