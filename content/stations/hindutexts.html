The two stories

All the Hindu texts, and how they fit

The Bible is one book with one story. Hindu tradition has many texts, written over about 3,000 years, with no single story and no closed list. This step is the map: what the texts are and how they fit together. The next steps take each group in turn and show where it breaks. Then comes the Bible's one story, and the two side by side.

The map

Hindus sort their texts into Shruti, "what was heard", the oldest and most respected, and Smriti, "what was remembered", which came later. A third group came later still: teachers explaining the older texts, manuals for temple worship, and devotional songs. Most Hindus do not rank these as Shruti or Smriti, but they shape how Hindus think and worship every day.

Only the Gita and a few teaching sections are part of the Mahabharata. The other texts on the map are separate works.

Ranked highest is not the same as read most

  • Ranked highest: the Vedas. On paper they have the final word, and when texts disagree, Shruti is meant to win. In practice they are mostly chanted in ritual, in Sanskrit, and rarely studied by ordinary Hindus.
  • Read and quoted most: the Gita. It ranks second, as Smriti, yet it is the text Hindus turn to for teaching, which is why this guide focuses on it.
  • Shapes daily worship most: the Puranas, the epics and devotional songs. These are the stories told at home, the festivals and the temple gods.

So there is no single Hindu answer to which text decides a question. The Bible has no such gap: the book that has the final word is the same book every Christian is meant to read, "profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction" (2 Timothy 3:16).

In time order

The dates below are scholars' estimates. Tradition places most of these texts far earlier.

  1. About 1500 to 1000 BCEThe Vedas

    Hymns and ritual instructions for the sacrifice.

  2. About 800 to 500 BCEThe Upanishads

    The philosophy of Brahman and Atman, and the first clear teaching of karma and rebirth.

  3. About 500 BCE to 400 CEThe Ramayana and the Mahabharata

    The two great epics, which grew over centuries. The Gita is inside the Mahabharata.

  4. About 200 BCE to 400 CELaw books and the Sutras

    The Manusmriti and other law books, the Brahma Sutras and the Yoga Sutras.

  5. About 300 to 1000 CEThe Puranas and temple manuals

    Stories of the gods, and the manuals for temple worship. The gods and the worship known today take their shape here.

  6. About 600 to 1700 CEDevotional poetry

    Poet-saints writing in everyday languages, from the Tamil Alvars to Tulsidas, Jnaneshwar and Tukaram.

How it fits the whole Hindu view

Many Hindus see the texts as three roads that lead to the same goal, which is release from the cycle of rebirth (moksha):

The way of action (karma-kanda)
Where it comes from

The rituals of the VedasDo your duty and the ritual acts correctly.

In plain words

Live rightlyKeep dharma, your duty, and offer your actions to the gods.

The way of knowledge (jnana-kanda)
Where it comes from

The UpanishadsLearn who you truly are and what ultimate reality is.

In plain words

See clearlyMoksha comes through understanding that the true self is one with Brahman.

The way of devotion
Where it comes from

The Gita, the epics and the PuranasLove and trust a personal God.

In plain words

Love and surrenderWorship with love and give yourself to the Lord.

The Gita is unusual because it brings all three together in one conversation, which is one reason it is so loved.

Four texts, four answers

Put side by side, each text answers a different question. This is why the Vedas and Upanishads are not another story beside the Mahabharata and the Bible. They are the ritual and the philosophy that sit underneath the Hindu side of the questions in this guide.

The Vedas
  • Problem Out of step with the gods and the order of the world.
  • Way out The right ritual.
  • Goal Blessing now, and heaven.
The Upanishads
  • Problem Ignorance of who you really are.
  • Way out Knowledge.
  • Goal Moksha, release from rebirth.
The Mahabharata and the Gita
  • Problem How to act rightly when duties collide.
  • Way out Do your duty without clinging to the result, and trust the Lord.
  • Goal Right action, and release.
The Bible
  • Problem Sin: a broken relationship with a holy God.
  • Way out God's rescue, through Jesus Christ.
  • Goal Forgiven, made new, and living with God.
  • Where you meet them later. The Upanishads are the source of the ideas in "Monism and the personal God", "Karma and the endless chain" and "Punished for a life you cannot remember". The Rig Veda is the source of "truth is one, the wise speak of it in many ways" in "Can every path be true?".
  • How it differs from the Bible. The Bible also has ritual (Leviticus), wisdom (Ecclesiastes) and law, but all inside one story of redemption. The Hindu texts give a different answer in each layer, and the schools disagree over how to combine them.

Christ above all

Among all the ways people have pictured God, one is different. God did not stay in a story or a symbol: He became a person, "full of grace and truth" (John 1:14), and He is good all the way through.
