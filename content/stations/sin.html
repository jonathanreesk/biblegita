Sin, forgiveness and the Lamb

Core difference: who pays

Hinduism does speak about sin (papa) and offers ways to be free of it. The difference is not whether sin is addressed, but how. In the Gita, sin is removed by knowledge, by duty, by rites or by devotion. In the gospel, sin is a debt against a holy God that someone must pay, and God pays it Himself.

The Hindu claim

The Gita does promise freedom from sin. Knowledge burns up karma like fire burns wood. Even the worst wrongdoer can cross over on the boat of knowledge. A devotee who turns to Krishna is to be counted righteous, and Krishna promises to release the one who surrenders from all sins.

The apologetic argument

  1. If sin is real wrong, justice requires that it be paid, not ignored.
  2. Karma gives justice with no mercy: the self pays everything.
  3. Krishna's promise gives mercy, but never says how justice is satisfied.
  4. Only the cross holds both: God punishes sin fully and forgives the sinner fully, because He bears the penalty Himself.

Expect this reply

  • "Krishna's grace is free too." Vaishnava traditions teach surrender (prapatti) and grace. Why does God need blood to forgive?
  • "Punishing an innocent person is unjust." Your own Ezekiel 18:20 says the son shall not bear the father's sin.

Your strongest answer

Forgiveness always costs the one who forgives. A judge who simply waives a crime is not merciful but corrupt; a judge who pays the fine himself is both just and merciful. The cross is not God punishing an unwilling third party. The Son lays down His life by His own choice (John 10:18), and God in Christ reconciles the world to Himself (2 Corinthians 5:19). The substitute is the Judge. That is what Krishna's promise in 18:66 leaves unexplained, and what the Lamb provides.

A careful bridge

The Rig Veda's Purusha Sukta (Rig Veda 10.90) describes a cosmic person sacrificed by the gods, from whom the world comes. Some Indian Christians use it as a starting point for talking about Christ. Use it with care: in the Veda the sacrifice creates the world and its order, it does not atone for sin. It shows the longing, not the answer.

Christ above all

Christ is both the Judge and the One who pays. He is the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world (John 1:29). There is no one else who can stand in your place, and no one who stands more gladly.
